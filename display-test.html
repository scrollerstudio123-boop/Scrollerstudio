Reading the results

Refresh rate reads lower than the spec

Browser throttling, power-saving settings, or a cable/port that doesn't support your monitor's full rate can all cap the number below the advertised spec.

A dot stays black on every color

This is a classic dead pixel — the pixel isn't receiving power at all.

A dot stays one color regardless of the swatch

This points to a stuck pixel — it's receiving power but stuck on red, green, or blue. Gentle massage techniques sometimes fix stuck pixels; dead pixels generally don't recover.

Faint uneven brightness on black

Usually backlight bleed rather than a pixel fault — common on LCD panels and generally not something a color test alone can distinguish from a true defect.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is the refresh rate measurement?

It's a solid estimate based on real rendered frames, but browser tab throttling and other running processes can shave a few Hz off the true panel rate — treat it as a strong approximation, not a certified reading.

Why does my laptop show a lower rate on battery power?

Many laptops automatically cap the display's refresh rate to save power when unplugged — check your power settings if the number seems low.

What's the difference between a dead pixel and a stuck pixel?

A dead pixel shows no light on any color, including white. A stuck pixel lights up but is locked to a single color no matter what's displayed behind it.
