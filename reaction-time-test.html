How to read your reaction time

RangeWhat it typically means
Under 200msElite reaction speed — faster than the vast majority of results reported on tests like this.
200–250msSolid, above-average reaction time, common among practiced gamers.
250–300msA typical, healthy adult reaction time under normal conditions.
300ms+Still normal, but can be pulled higher by tiredness, distraction, or an input device with extra lag.

What actually affects this number

Display lag

The time between the color actually changing and it appearing on your screen adds directly to your measured reaction time, and varies by monitor.

Mouse and browser lag

Wireless mice, low polling rates, and background browser tabs can all add a few extra milliseconds before your click registers.

Anticipation vs. reaction

Clicking based on a guessed rhythm rather than the actual color change will occasionally produce false, too-fast "reactions" that don't reflect real reflexes — the test resets if you click too early to guard against this.

Fatigue and focus

Reaction time reliably gets worse with tiredness and distraction, which is why testing across several attempts gives a more honest picture than a single click.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my test reset when I clicked?

You clicked before the box turned green. This is treated as a false start and doesn't count as a valid attempt, since it wouldn't reflect a genuine reaction.

Is this an accurate, lab-grade measurement?

It's accurate enough for casual comparison and tracking your own trend over time, but browser timing, display refresh rate, and input lag all add small amounts of noise that a dedicated lab setup would control for.

Why does my reaction time vary so much between attempts?

Natural variation of 30–80ms between attempts is normal. Look at your average across several tries rather than your single best result for a more realistic number.

Does screen size or resolution affect the result?

Not directly, but a lower refresh-rate monitor can add a few extra milliseconds before the color change is actually visible, which will show up in your recorded time.
